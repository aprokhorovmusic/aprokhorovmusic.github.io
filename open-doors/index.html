Music of Open Doors

One concert, one night, one tape that sat on a shelf for sixteen years. Two classical guitars, hand percussion and flutes, restored and released for the first time.

The concert

On 26 May 2010 an acoustic ensemble called Crystal Ship played a single concert in a hall in Tver. The program, as printed on the handbill, promised "psychedelic poetry, atonal music and assorted nonsense." Nine of the twelve pieces are the duo's own and three of those were improvised on stage that night. The set also carries a piece by their teacher Evgeny Baev, Carlo Domeniconi's Koyunbaba and Villa-Lobos's Etude No. 8.

The name is a pun the ensemble had been sitting on since 1967: the group is called Crystal Ship, so the evening was billed as the music of open Doors.

It sounds like ritual music rather than a recital: nylon strings, ethnic flutes and hand percussion in a long reverb, avant-garde in shape, with three of the twelve pieces invented on the spot.
